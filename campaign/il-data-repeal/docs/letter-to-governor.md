Dear Governor Pritzker,

I am writing to respectfully request your support for the repeal of the Digital Asset Tax Act (Public Act 104-0468) through **House Bill 5798**, or alternatively, your consideration of amendatory action to prevent this law from taking effect on January 1, 2027.

I recognize that Illinois faces genuine fiscal challenges, and I respect the difficult work of balancing the state's budget. Revenue matters, and I do not write to suggest otherwise. I write because I believe this particular provision will ultimately **cost Illinois more revenue than it generates** — and because there is still time to prevent that outcome.

### What the Law Does

The Digital Asset Tax imposes a 0.2% privilege tax on the **total value** of digital asset transactions. Unlike capital gains taxes — which Illinois already applies to cryptocurrency profits through the income tax — this levy applies to the entire transaction amount regardless of whether the user earned any profit.

To illustrate: if a resident buys $10,000 in cryptocurrency and later sells it for $8,000 — a $2,000 loss — they still owe $36 in Digital Asset Tax (0.2% on the buy and the sell). No other asset class in Illinois is treated this way. Stocks, bonds, and commodity futures are taxed on gains, not on the act of trading itself.

### Why This Approach Is Counterproductive

**The revenue projection is modest; the downside risk is large.** The estimated $60 million in annual revenue represents approximately 0.11% of the state's budget. Meanwhile, the digital asset industry in Illinois — including companies, events, and communities that generate jobs, sales tax revenue, hotel tax revenue, and income tax revenue — represents a far larger economic footprint.

**Illinois is the first and only state to impose a transaction-level tax on digital assets.** This distinction is not a competitive advantage. It is a warning sign to every crypto company, developer, and event organizer evaluating where to invest. States like Texas, Florida, Wyoming, and Colorado have taken the opposite approach, actively recruiting the industry that Illinois is now taxing.

**The federal regulatory landscape is evolving rapidly.** Federal agencies including the CFTC and SEC are developing comprehensive frameworks for digital asset oversight. CFTC Chair Selig has publicly characterized state transaction taxes on digital assets as punitive "sin taxes." A state-level transaction tax risks conflict with emerging federal standards and potential preemption challenges.

**Community organizations are already making contingency plans.** OnionDAO, a month-long technology gathering that brings 400+ people and significant economic activity to Chicago annually, is evaluating whether to continue holding its event in Illinois. Other conferences, meetups, and industry gatherings face similar calculations. The economic impact of these events — restaurant spending, venue rentals, accommodation, transportation — already generates substantial tax revenue for the state through existing mechanisms.

### What I Am Asking

I respectfully ask that you consider one of the following courses of action:

1. **Support HB 5798** — Rep. Cabello's clean repeal bill — and encourage its advancement during the fall veto session
2. **Issue an amendatory veto** modifying the Digital Asset Tax to apply only to realized gains (aligning it with how every other asset class is treated), if full repeal is not feasible
3. **Direct the Department of Revenue** to delay implementation and conduct a thorough economic impact study before the tax takes effect

Any of these actions would demonstrate that Illinois takes innovation seriously while still maintaining the state's ability to generate fair revenue from all economic activity.

### A Constructive Path Forward

I am not asking for the crypto industry to be exempt from taxation. Illinois residents already pay state income tax on cryptocurrency profits — as they should. What I am asking is that digital assets receive the **same treatment** as stocks, bonds, and other financial instruments: taxed on gains, not on the act of transacting.

This is not a partisan issue. The original SB 3019 passed with votes from both parties, and the concerns about its impact cross party lines. The goal is sound fiscal policy that generates sustainable revenue without driving a growing industry out of the state.
