# Testimony Templates — Repeal the Digital Asset Tax

## How to Use This Document

Two templates below: a **2-minute spoken testimony** for town halls and committee hearings, and a **longer written testimony** for submission to legislators and committees. Customize with your personal details and story.

### Tips for Spoken Testimony

1. **Be personal, not technical.** Legislators hear policy arguments all day. They remember the person who told them about their family, their business, their community.

2. **Mention your district.** "I live in [city] in your district" tells them you're a voter.

3. **One story, one ask.** Don't try to cover every argument. Pick the angle that's most authentic to you and commit to it.

4. **Practice the bank account analogy.** It's the single most effective way to explain why 0.2% on total value is different from a capital gains tax: "Imagine paying a tax every time you moved money between your own bank accounts."

5. **Bring a specific number.** "This will cost me $X per year" or "Our event brings $X to Chicago" is more powerful than "this is bad for the economy."

6. **Stay calm and respectful.** Even if the legislator is hostile or dismissive. Other people in the room are watching.

7. **Don't read verbatim.** Use the template as a guide, but speak naturally. Eye contact matters.

8. **Dress for credibility.** You don't need a suit, but look like someone whose opinion should be taken seriously.

9. **Arrive early.** Sign up to speak first if possible. Early speakers get more attention.

10. **Bring friends.** Even if they don't speak, bodies in the room signal that people care about this issue.

## Version 2: Written Testimony (Committee Submission)

**RE: Support for HB 5798 — Repeal of the Digital Asset Tax Act**

[DATE]

Dear Chairman Tarver and Members of the House Revenue & Finance Committee,

My name is [YOUR NAME], and I am a resident of [CITY, STATE ZIP] in Illinois's [Xth] House District. I am writing to urge the committee to advance **HB 5798**, which would repeal the Digital Asset Tax Act (Public Act 104-0468) before its January 1, 2027 effective date.

### Personal Impact

[2-3 paragraphs about how the tax affects you personally. Be specific about dollar amounts, lifestyle changes, or business decisions you would have to make. Use the same angles as the spoken testimony but with more detail.]

### Why This Tax Is Different

The Digital Asset Tax is a **0.2% privilege tax on the total transaction value** of digital asset transfers — not on profits or gains. No other asset class in Illinois is taxed this way:

- Stock trades: taxed on capital gains only
- Bond transactions: taxed on capital gains only
- Real estate transfers: transfer tax applies, but not on every interim transaction
- Bank transfers: no transaction tax at all

A person who buys $10,000 of cryptocurrency, watches it lose value, and sells for $8,000 would owe **$36 in Digital Asset Tax** (0.2% on the buy and the sell) despite **losing $2,000**. A person who makes the same trade with stocks owes nothing until they realize a gain.

This is not equitable taxation. It is a discriminatory levy on a single technology.

### Economic Consequences

[1-2 paragraphs about the broader economic impact. Reference the OnionDAO economic impact data, industry flight risk, and comparison to other states' approaches. See `economic-impact.md` for specific numbers.]

The projected revenue of approximately $60 million per year represents **0.11% of the state's $53 billion budget**. This marginal revenue will be more than offset by:

- Loss of crypto industry jobs and tax base
- Relocation of events, conferences, and community gatherings to other states
- Reduced competitiveness for Illinois in attracting technology companies
- Legal challenges that will consume state resources (see Commerce Clause, Internet Tax Freedom Act, and Equal Protection concerns)

### The Federal Context

CFTC Chair Christy Goldsmith Romero Selig has publicly characterized state-level digital asset transaction taxes as akin to "sin taxes" — penalizing participation in a legitimate, federally regulated market. Federal legislation and regulatory frameworks are actively evolving, and Illinois risks being the cautionary example of how not to approach digital asset policy.

### My Ask

I respectfully request that the committee:

1. Schedule HB 5798 for a hearing at the earliest opportunity
2. Advance the bill to the full House for a vote during the fall veto session
3. Engage with the digital asset community in Illinois before implementing any future regulatory or tax framework

Thank you for your time and consideration. I am available to provide additional testimony or answer questions.

Respectfully,

[YOUR NAME]
[ADDRESS]
[EMAIL]
[PHONE]

### Tips for Written Testimony

1. **Keep it under 2 pages.** Staff summarize longer submissions and may lose your key points.

2. **Lead with who you are and where you live.** District residents get more weight.

3. **Include specific dollar amounts.** Vague concerns are easy to dismiss.

4. **Reference the bill number (HB 5798)** in your subject line and first paragraph.

5. **Be respectful of the committee's time.** Acknowledge that revenue is a legitimate concern, then explain why this particular approach is counterproductive.

6. **Send to all committee members**, not just your own representative. Addresses available in `coalition-contacts.md`.

7. **CC your own representative** even if they're not on the committee.

8. **Follow up.** A written submission followed by a phone call is more effective than either alone.
