## How to Walk Into a District Office

**Yes, you can literally walk in.** District offices are public offices staffed during business hours (typically Mon-Fri 9am-5pm, but call first).

### The Script (walking in cold):

> "Hi, my name is Deric Ortiz. I'm a constituent — I live in [your city/neighborhood]. I'm here because I'm concerned about the Digital Asset Tax in SB 3019 and I'd like to share why it matters to me.
>
> Is the Representative available? If not, could I speak with someone on staff about this issue? I'd also like to leave a written statement."

### What happens:
1. **Best case:** You get a meeting with the Rep. Bring your testimony (see below).
2. **Likely case:** You meet with a legislative aide / district director. **This is still valuable.** These staffers brief the Rep and their opinion matters.
3. **Minimum case:** You leave a written statement and your contact info. They log constituent contacts by issue — volume matters.

### What to bring:
- A printed copy of your one-page testimony (see script below)
- A printed copy of the one-pager (`docs/one-pager.md`)
- Your ID (to prove you're a constituent — driver's license with your address)
- A business card if you have one

## Your 2-Minute Town Hall Testimony

*Practice this out loud. Time yourself. Cut anything over 2 minutes.*

> Thank you, Representative [NAME]. My name is Deric Ortiz. I'm from [CITY/NEIGHBORHOOD].
>
> I'm here about the Digital Asset Tax Act in SB 3019. I want to tell you about something this tax is going to kill.
>
> Last month I attended OnionDAO — a month-long open community event in Chicago funded by people in the crypto and Solana ecosystem. It was free. Open to anyone. There were workshops to learn coding, hardware, AI. Free coworking space. Free lunch. Concerts on the river. They bought out entire restaurants to feed attendees.
>
> I gave a workshop on AI development. A kid named Rizzy — he couldn't have been more than fourteen — taught me how to solder. At the end, there was a gifting ceremony where attendees nominated people who'd impacted them and shared their stories. People of all ages, all walks of life, all skill levels.
>
> The organizer told me he may not do OnionDAO anymore because of this tax. Not because of the rate — because the structure. 0.2% on every transaction. Not on profits. On total value. Every time digital assets move. If you run a DAO treasury, if you use smart contracts, if you just move tokens between your own wallets — taxed. Every single time.
>
> That's not how we tax stocks. It's not how we tax bonds. It's not how we tax bank transfers. This only targets crypto. And the people it drives out are the ones building community here.
>
> I'm asking you to support HB 5798 and repeal this tax before it takes effect on January 1st.
>
> Thank you.

## Quick Reference: What to Say

### On the phone:
> "Hi, my name is Deric Ortiz. I'm a [constituent / Chicago resident]. I'm calling about the Digital Asset Tax in SB 3019. I'm asking Representative [NAME] to support HB 5798, which would repeal this tax. This tax is threatening community events like OnionDAO that bring hundreds of people to Chicago for free workshops and education. Can you tell me the Representative's position on this issue?"

### In an email:
> Subject: Constituent Request — Support HB 5798 to Repeal the Digital Asset Tax
>
> Dear Representative [NAME],
>
> [Use the town hall request email template above, but add your personal story in 2-3 sentences]

### On social media (@-ing a legislator):
> .@[LegislatorHandle] The IL Digital Asset Tax (SB 3019) isn't a tax on crypto profits — it's 0.2% on EVERY transaction's total value. It's driving community builders out of Chicago. Please support HB 5798 to repeal. #RepealDATA

### When someone says "it's only 0.2%":
> "Imagine your bank charged 0.2% of your total balance every time you moved money between accounts, sent money to a friend, or just kept it in savings. In crypto, assets move constantly — smart contracts, DeFi, DAO operations. 0.2% on every move compounds into thousands of dollars. It's not a capital gains tax. It's a toll on every heartbeat of the network."

### When someone says "we need the revenue":
> "The tax is projected to raise $60 million. Illinois's total budget is $56 billion. That's 0.1% of the budget. Meanwhile, the crypto industry has poured $8.6 million into Illinois races and events like OnionDAO bring hundreds of thousands of dollars in direct spending to Chicago. You don't raise $60 million by driving out the people who spend hundreds of millions in your state."
