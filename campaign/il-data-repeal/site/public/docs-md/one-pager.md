# Repeal the Illinois Digital Asset Tax (DATA)

## What Is DATA?

The **Digital Asset Tax Act** (Public Act 104-0468, originally SB 3019) imposes a **0.2% privilege tax on the total value of every digital asset transaction** conducted by Illinois residents or through Illinois-based exchanges. Governor Pritzker signed it into law on **June 16, 2026**. It takes effect **January 1, 2027**.

This is not a tax on profits. It is a tax on the **entire transaction amount**, every single time.

## The Bank Account Analogy

Imagine if every time you moved money between your own bank accounts — checking to savings, savings to investment — the state took 0.2% of the total amount you moved. Not 0.2% of what you earned. 0.2% of everything you touched.

- Move $10,000 from checking to savings? That costs you $20.
- Move it back a week later? Another $20.
- Do this monthly? You lose $480/year just for managing your own money.

That is exactly what DATA does to digital asset users. Every swap, every transfer, every DeFi interaction — taxed on the full amount, regardless of whether you made or lost money.

## Death by a Thousand Cuts

For active DeFi users, the math is devastating:

- A single token swap on a decentralized exchange may involve 3-5 underlying transactions (approve, swap, route through liquidity pools)
- Each one is taxed at 0.2% of the total value
- A user making 10 swaps per month on $5,000 positions loses **$500-1,200/year** — even if their portfolio is flat or down
- Liquidity providers, yield farmers, and developers are hit hardest

The projected revenue is roughly **$60 million/year** — a rounding error in Illinois's $53 billion budget. The cost: driving an entire industry and its economic activity out of state.

## Who Is Affected?

- **Individual crypto holders** who use any exchange or DeFi protocol
- **Illinois-based exchanges and trading platforms** (who must collect and remit)
- **DeFi developers and projects** building in Illinois
- **Event organizers** like OnionDAO who bring crypto communities (and their spending) to Chicago
- **Small businesses** that accept crypto payments
- **Anyone** who interacts with digital assets while residing in Illinois

## What We Want

**Full repeal of the Digital Asset Tax Act via HB 5798**, filed by Rep. John M. Cabello (R-68). The bill is two sentences:

> *"The Digital Asset Tax Act is repealed. Effective immediately."*
> — HB 5798, 104th General Assembly (35 ILCS 195/Act rep.)

## Why Repeal, Not Amend?

- The tax structure is fundamentally flawed — taxing transaction volume rather than gains
- No other asset class (stocks, bonds, commodities, real estate) is taxed this way in Illinois
- Amendment would still leave a discriminatory framework in place
- Other states are actively recruiting the businesses and talent Illinois is pushing away

## OnionDAO Already Generates Tax Revenue for Chicago

The crypto community doesn't just take — it spends. OnionDAO alone: 30 days of catered meals (10.25% sales tax), restaurant buyouts, hotel stays for out-of-towners (19% — highest in the US), venue rentals (9% commercial lease tax), concerts (9% amusement tax). That's existing tax revenue at risk.

Chicago just raised hotel taxes to 19% and created a $40M/year fund specifically to attract conventions and events. OnionDAO came for free and brought 400 people for a month. The state is now taxing away the industry that funds it.
