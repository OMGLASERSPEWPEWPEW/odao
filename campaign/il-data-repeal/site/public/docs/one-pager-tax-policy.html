To: Office of Representative Jaime Andrade Jr. From: Deric Ortiz — Onion DAO Date: July 6, 2026 Subject: The Case Against the Digital Asset Privilege Tax (SB 3019)

The Core Issue: Taxing the Medium, Not the Substance

The 0.02% Digital Asset Privilege Tax reflects a foundational misunderstanding of blockchain technology. It does not tax income, capital gains, or profits. Instead, it places a punitive fee on the basic act of using a digital network. If an Illinois resident trades a stock or derivative in traditional markets, there is no state financial transaction tax. If that exact same asset is transacted on a blockchain, it is taxed. This targets the medium rather than the substance, penalizing the technology itself.

No Carve-Outs for Basic Utility

Because the tax targets the pure mechanics of digital asset business activity — defined broadly as exchanging, transferring, or storing — it captures routine, non-commercial activity. There are no meaningful carve-outs for individuals moving their own digital assets between personal wallets or utilizing decentralized protocols for basic custody. It is the equivalent of taxing a citizen every time they move a twenty-dollar bill from their left pocket to their right pocket.

The Economic Reality: Sacrificing the Future for $60 Million

The state estimated this tax would generate approximately $60 million. In the context of a $55.9 billion state budget, this revenue can and should be found elsewhere. Illinois is now the only state in the country to impose a transaction-based tax on digital assets. By doing so, the state is actively driving developers, startups, and capital across state lines. Illinois is trading its future as a national tech hub for a marginal, short-term budget line item.

The Ask: A Clean Repeal

We are not asking for special treatment or regulatory loopholes; we are asking for foundational parity. The state must recognize that this tax is structurally unworkable. While Republican messaging bills like HB 5798 have been filed to score political points, we urge Representative Andrade and Democratic leadership to support a genuine, bipartisan effort to fully repeal this provision and protect the state's innovation economy.
